# Resume Guidelines for Engineering Leadership Roles

## Structure

- Lead with a 2-3 sentence summary: level of leadership, domain expertise, scale of impact, and what you're looking for.
- Order sections: Summary, Experience, Technical Skills, Education. Skills before education — hiring managers care more about what you can do.
- Each role gets 4-6 bullets max. More dilutes impact.
- Most recent role gets the most real estate. Anything older than 10 years gets 1-2 bullets.

## Writing Bullets

- Every bullet follows: **Action verb + what you did + measurable result**.
- Quantify everything: team size, revenue impact, latency reduction, uptime, adoption rates, cost savings.
- Distinguish between "led" (you owned it), "drove" (you influenced it), and "contributed to" (you helped). Don't inflate.
- Start bullets with strong verbs: Architected, Shipped, Scaled, Led, Owned, Built, Reduced, Accelerated. Avoid weak verbs: Helped, Assisted, Participated, Supported.
- Show scope progression across roles — from individual contributor to team lead to org leader.

## Leadership Signals

- State the number of engineers you managed (direct + total org), and whether you managed managers.
- Mention cross-functional work: product, design, SRE, security, legal, compliance.
- Include hiring and team-building: "Built team from 5 to 25" is more compelling than "Managed a team of 25."
- Show strategic impact: roadmap ownership, technical vision docs, architecture decisions that shaped the org.
- Include mentorship outcomes where possible: promotions you sponsored, engineers who grew into leads.

## Technical Credibility

- Engineering leaders are expected to be technical. Show hands-on contributions even in senior roles.
- Name specific technologies, frameworks, and systems you architected or made decisions about.
- Include architecture decisions and their trade-offs: "Chose X over Y because Z, resulting in..."
- Cloud platforms, infrastructure patterns, and system design at scale are high-signal for leadership roles.

## Tailoring per Role

- Mirror the job posting's language. If they say "platform engineering," don't call it "infrastructure."
- Move the most relevant experience and skills to the top of each section.
- If the role emphasizes a domain (AI, security, identity), ensure your summary and top bullets reflect that domain.
- Add domain-specific protocols and standards to your skills section only if you've actually used them.

## Common Mistakes

- Listing responsibilities instead of achievements. "Responsible for CI/CD pipeline" says nothing. "Reduced deploy time from 45 min to 8 min by rebuilding CI/CD on GitHub Actions" says everything.
- Burying impact in the middle of a bullet. Lead with the result or put it at the end — never in the middle.
- Using internal jargon or project codenames that mean nothing outside your company.
- Making the resume longer than 2 pages. If you have 15+ years of experience, be more selective, not more verbose.
- Listing every technology you've ever touched. Curate the skills section to match the target role.

## Formatting

- One page for < 10 years experience, two pages for 10+. Never three.
- Consistent date format throughout (e.g., "Jan 2022 - Present").
- No objective statements, no "References available upon request," no headshots.
- Use a clean, ATS-friendly format: no tables, no columns, no graphics. Plain sections with clear headings.
- PDF for submission. The filename should be: FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf.
